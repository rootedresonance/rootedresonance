The Framework/Reconnect
Stage III of Five

Reconnect

Learn to trust your body and nervous system.

IAwakenNoticeIIUnderstandRecognizeIIIReconnectTrustIVBecomeDiscoverVRelateChoose

Connection with your body begins with experiencing that which we have learned to disconnect from. Recovery from survival states means traveling away from the mind and allowing the body to have a voice.

Reconnection is the practice of returning. Learning to breathe. To pause. To notice.

To trust that your body carries wisdom, rather than something to overcome.

What is happening

As you begin to build the bridge of connection between you and your body, things begin to feel more regulated. Suddenly the highs don't feel so high and the lows don't feel so low.

The body becomes a place to inhabit rather than avoid. Healing begins to shift from intellectual understanding into lived experience.

How we work together

In this stage we are all about the body. The tools offered here have less to do with verbal processing and more to do with somatic experience.

Through breathwork, nervous system education, sound healing, mindfulness, and embodied practices, you'll develop a deeper relationship with yourself from the inside out.

What disconnection actually feels like

Usually not dramatic. More often it shows up as not knowing you are hungry until you are irritable, or not registering that you are exhausted until you sit down. Feeling described rather than felt. Being able to report on your emotions accurately while experiencing them at a distance.

This is not a deficiency. It is something the body learned to do because feeling everything at the time would have been too much. The work here is not to force the connection open but to make it safe enough to return gradually.

What this looks like in practice

  • Noticing where a sentence lands physically, and staying there rather than moving on.
  • Learning your own signals of activation and shutdown, so you can read them earlier.
  • Breath practices short enough to actually use, rather than ones that require a free hour.
  • Sound, where nothing is asked of you and nothing has to be explained.
  • Finding out what settles you specifically, which is often not what the internet suggests.

If you cannot feel anything at first

Many people cannot, and arrive convinced this means the work will not take. It does not mean that. Numbness is information, not an absence of it, and the answer "I do not notice anything" is a real answer rather than a wrong one.

Pace matters more here than anywhere else. Going faster than your system can tolerate does not speed anything up. It teaches the body that this was right to be wary of.
