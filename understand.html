The Framework/Understand
Stage II of Five

Understand

Recognize the adaptations that once protected you.

IAwakenNoticeIIUnderstandRecognizeIIIReconnectTrustIVBecomeDiscoverVRelateChoose

As awareness grows, so does compassion.

What once felt like a reflex suddenly gets pulled into the light. The patterns that once defined you, people pleaser, hyperindependent, the person who never says no, now become known as strategies for survival.

They developed as adaptations to your relationships, environment, and experiences.

Instead of asking, what's wrong with me, you begin asking, what was I trying to protect?

What is happening

Your nervous system is beginning to separate the past from the present. As you understand how your adaptations formed, you gain the freedom to choose rather than simply react.

Insight creates possibilities, but lasting change also requires embodiment.

How we work together

We will explore all the things that keep you feeling stuck. Whether it's relationship patterns, feeling like you don't know who you are, or deep generational beliefs that have been unconsciously leading your life.

Here we build understanding without shame, and without treating you as something to be fixed.

Why insight is not the same as change

Most people who reach this stage are already articulate about themselves. They have read the books, done the therapy, and can trace the pattern to its origin. And they are still doing the thing.

That gap is the most common reason people come to me, and it is not a failure of effort. Understanding is stored in one system. The pattern runs in another, older one, which learned its job before you had language and has no particular interest in your analysis. You cannot argue a nervous system out of a conclusion it reached in order to keep you safe.

The strategies, and why they held

  • People pleasing, which kept the peace when the peace was fragile.
  • Hyperindependence, which meant never being disappointed by anyone.
  • Reading a room before entering it, which used to be necessary information.
  • Never saying no, because the cost of no was once too high.
  • Explaining a feeling instead of having it, which made unbearable things manageable.

None of these are flaws. Each one worked. The difficulty is that a strategy which worked has no mechanism for noticing that the conditions have changed.

What we actually do here

We slow down at the moment the pattern fires rather than analysing it afterward. That means noticing what happens in your body when the old response begins, and staying with it long enough to find out what it is protecting. It is less satisfying than a good explanation and considerably more useful.
